\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We presented EventGS, a framework for event-driven online dynamic 3D Gaussian Splatting from a static event camera given a known baseline scene---a digital-twin / scene-live-update setting unexplored by prior event-3DGS work. By decoupling fidelity (baseline $\Gzero$) from temporal resolution (events), and by coupling events to Gaussians through a differentiable event-consistency loss with bidirectional supervision, the approach enables high-temporal-resolution dynamic reconstruction. The central technical contribution is making the aperture degeneracy of event-only reconstruction \emph{computable}: a per-Gaussian observability score from the structure tensor of the baseline render that both weights the event loss away from the degenerate minimum and adaptively schedules keyframes. A Contrast-Maximization regularizer on the predicted 2D Gaussian flow provides a keyframe-free, edge-normal aperture-breaking signal in every window. The prototype validates the formulation on three synthetic benchmarks isolating the aperture problem, showing correct-sign motion recovery where event-only recovery is at chance, at $\sim$$100\times$ lower keyframe cadence than fixed-cadence alignment. Extending to real event cameras, larger scenes, and the full CUDA 4D rasterizer---and integrating recent event-camera pose-estimation methods for fully known-pose deployment---is the natural next step.
